## What each business drive needs

IRS Publication 463 lists what your records must show for car expenses (Table 5-1, "How To Prove Certain Business Expenses"):

| Element | What to record for a car |
|---|---|
| Amount | The cost of the car and any improvements, the date you started using it for business, the mileage for each business use, and the total miles for the year |
| Time | The date of each use of the car |
| Place | Your business destination |
| Business purpose | The business purpose of the drive |

The publication is blunt about guesses: "You can't deduct amounts that you approximate or estimate."

A log line that covers every element for one drive looks like this:

`2026-11-10 · Home → Client office, 120 Main St · 12.4 mi · Business: site visit`

## Paper or app: both count

You can keep the record "in an account book, diary, log, statement of expense, trip sheets, or similar record." It generally has to be written, because written evidence is more reliable than memory, and Publication 463 says that "if you prepare a record on a computer, it is considered an adequate record." A mileage app's log is a computer record.

## Write it down at or near the time

The IRS asks you to record each business use "at or near the time" and explains why: "A timely kept record has more value than a statement prepared later when there is generally a lack of accurate recall."

You don't have to write every drive down the same day. Publication 463 says that if you keep a log on a weekly basis that accounts for use during the week, the log counts as a timely kept record. A log rebuilt from memory at tax time is exactly what this rule is meant to avoid.

## When the purpose is obvious

You must generally write down the business purpose, but not when it's clear from the circumstances. Publication 463's example is a sales representative on an established sales route: they can record the length of the route once, the date of each trip at or near the time, and the total miles driven that year.

Several uses of the car that are really one, such as a round trip or a delivery route, can go in a single record. A short personal stop, such as lunch between two business stops, doesn't interrupt the business use.

## Logging part of the year (sampling)

You can keep an adequate record for parts of the year and use it to prove your business use for the whole year, if you can show with other evidence that those periods are representative. The publication's example: a log kept for the first week of every month shows 75% business use, and invoices show the same pattern in the other weeks.

## Commuting doesn't count

Publication 463: "You can't deduct commuting expenses no matter how far your home is from your regular place of work. You can't deduct commuting expenses even if you work during the commuting trip." Taking business calls or talking business with a passenger doesn't change that.

The exceptions it lists:

- **Temporary work location.** If you have one or more regular work locations away from home, you can deduct the round trip between home and a temporary work location in the same trade or business, regardless of distance. A temporary work location is one where the work is realistically expected to last, and does last, one year or less. If you have no regular place of business, only temporary work locations outside your metropolitan area qualify.
- **Home office.** If your home office qualifies as your principal place of business, you can deduct trips between home and another work location in the same trade or business.
- **No regular office and no home office.** Your first business contact of the day inside your metropolitan area counts as your office: the drive from home to it, and from the last contact back home, is commuting. Driving from one client or customer to another is deductible.

Parking at your own workplace is a commuting cost. Business-related parking fees and tolls can be deducted on top of the standard mileage rate.

## What Schedule C asks about your car

If you're self-employed and claim car expenses on Schedule C (Form 1040) without filing Form 4562, Part IV asks:

- When you placed the vehicle in service for business (line 43).
- Of the total miles you drove it that year, how many were business, commuting and other (line 44).
- Whether it was available for personal use off duty, and whether you have another vehicle for personal use (lines 45 and 46).
- Whether you have evidence to support the deduction, and whether that evidence is written (lines 47a and 47b).

A log that records personal drives as well as business ones, and the car's total miles for the year, answers line 44 directly.

## Standard mileage rates by date

The IRS changed its rates in the middle of 2026, so the rate depends on the date of each drive:

| Miles driven | Business | Medical and military moving | Charity | IRS source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 70¢ | 21¢ | 14¢ | IR-2024-312 |
| January 1 to June 30, 2026 | 72.5¢ | 20.5¢ | 14¢ | IR-2025-128 |
| From July 1, 2026 | 76¢ | 23.5¢ | 14¢ | Announcement 2026-11 |

Announcement 2026-11, in Internal Revenue Bulletin 2026-29, raised the rates for miles driven on or after July 1, 2026 and gives the reason: "This modification results from recent increases in the price of fuel." The charity rate is set by law, which is why it stayed at 14¢. So 1,000 business miles driven in June 2026 are worth $725, and 1,000 driven in July $760.

Rules that come with the standard mileage rate, from Publication 463:

- For a car you own, you must choose the standard mileage rate in the first year the car is available for use in your business. In later years you can use either method. For a leased car, you must use it for the whole lease.
- You can't use it if you use five or more cars at the same time (as in fleet operations), or after claiming certain depreciation on the car (for example MACRS, a section 179 deduction or the special depreciation allowance).
- If you use the standard mileage rate for a year, you can't also deduct that year's actual car expenses, such as depreciation, lease payments, gas, oil, repairs, insurance or registration.
- Business parking fees and tolls are deductible in addition.

## How long to keep the log

Keep records that support a deduction for as long as they may be needed, which generally means 3 years from the date you file the return that claims it (a return filed early counts as filed on its due date). If you depreciate the car, keep the records of its business use for each year of the recovery period. Publication 583 explains record retention in more detail.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does the IRS require in a mileage log?

For each business use of the car: the date, your business destination, the business purpose and the miles. You also need the car's total miles for the year and the date you started using it for business. IRS Publication 463, Table 5-1, lists these elements.

### Is a mileage tracking app acceptable to the IRS?

IRS Publication 463 says a record prepared on a computer is considered an adequate record. What matters is that the log shows every required element and was kept at or near the time of each drive.

### Can I reconstruct a mileage log at the end of the year?

The IRS gives more weight to a record kept at or near the time than to one prepared later, and you can't deduct amounts you approximate or estimate. If your records are incomplete, you must back an element up with your own specific statement plus other supporting evidence. A weekly log counts as timely.

### Are drives from home to work deductible?

Not when they're between home and your main or regular place of work: the IRS treats those as personal commuting, however far the drive. Exceptions include trips to a temporary work location, and trips from a home office that's your principal place of business to another work location.

### What is the IRS mileage rate for the second half of 2026?

76 cents a mile for business, 23.5 cents for medical and military moving, and 14 cents for charity, for miles driven from July 1, 2026 (IRS Announcement 2026-11). From January 1 to June 30, 2026 the business rate was 72.5 cents.

### How long should I keep my mileage log?

Generally 3 years from the date you file the return that claims the deduction, and longer if you depreciate the car: then keep the business-use records for each year of its recovery period.
